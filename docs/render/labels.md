# Rotated labels

Read before changing rotated-label shaping, placement, fit, or hit testing.

[Guide](../render.md) · Paths in code examples are relative to the repo root.

## Rotated labels (`rotated_label/` + `shaders/rotated_label.wgsl`)

- **awl draws text at ONE angle unless a label asks otherwise.** glyphon 0.11 carries no transform anywhere — `TextArea` exposes `left/top/scale/bounds/default_color/custom_glyphs`, `CustomGlyph` exposes `left/top/width/height`, and there is no rotation, skew or matrix in the crate — so every run the document layer draws is upright and a world wanting a turned or slanted cue has no way to say so. `rotated_label` is that capability and only that: the caller shapes ONE short run into a `GlyphBuffer` (whichever face, size and colour its theme data names), `LabelMask::compose` composes that run's whole coverage into ONE R8 image on the CPU — the same swash cache glyphon rasterises from — and `RotatedLabelPipeline` paints it through ONE quad whose vertices are rotated onto a unit axis. The rotation is the axis rotation `shaders/caret.wgsl` already performs for the caret streak, applied to a glyph mask instead of a rounded rect; `SelectionPipeline::prepare_rotated` was the same step for selection quads.
- **It cannot become a second prose renderer.** The mask reads `layout_runs().next()` and stops, so there is no line breaking, no per-line alignment, no wrapping and no selection — a paragraph is structurally unreachable and the document layer stays the one prose renderer. The pipeline draws one label; a second is a second instance, not a longer buffer.
- **The frame is pure and separately owned** (`rotated_label/geometry.rs`): `label_axis_deg` (0° reads left to right, 90° bottom to top — screen pixels are y-down, so a reader's counter-clockwise turn is a negative one, and the four quadrant axes are returned EXACTLY rather than through the transcendentals, which is what makes a quarter turn a lossless texel transpose), `label_point`/`label_local` (the run's frame on and off the screen), `label_quad`, `label_bounds` (the TIGHT axis-aligned extent, for reserving a gutter or proving non-overlap) and `label_hit` (the rotated box, NOT the bounds, which at a slant over-claims its own corners). `unit_axis` is the one normaliser both the geometry and `prepare` route through, so the CPU's measurements and the GPU's quad cannot disagree; it degenerates to the shared inert `selection::UPRIGHT_AXIS` on a zero/NaN input.
- **Quality is MEASURED.** At 0/90/180/270° the transform is lossless: the composed coverage arrives with ink mass 1.0000, mean absolute error 0.0000 and full stroke/gap contrast, at 1× and 2×. At an arbitrary slant it is one bilinear resample and nothing more — the rendered canvas matches an ideal bilinear rigid rotation computed independently on the CPU to within 0.005 of full coverage, so the softening a slant costs is the unavoidable price of rotating a raster, not ink the pipeline loses. The composed mask carries a one-pixel transparent border because a rotated quad has no anti-aliasing of its own and would otherwise cut a stem square at its boundary. Laws: `rotated_label::tests` (the frame, oracled by polygon winding and measured side lengths) and `render/tests/rotated_label.rs` (the pixels, oracled by the CPU-composed coverage image, which no rotation code touches).
- A per-instance colour PAIR interpolates along the run's own baseline, so a world whose visual language is a gradient line gets one without a second code path; equal values are a flat label. Nothing here reads a theme — which string, which axis, which colours, where, is theme data a caller supplies.
- **Two world expressions share ONE preparation owner** (`render/rotated_location.rs`'s `TextPipeline::prepare_rotated_location_label`, dispatched by `render/chrome/rotated_location.rs` on `theme::LocationStyle`): Cassowary's `RotatedRail` (90°, composed against the ROOM — see the paragraph below) and Magpie's `Raked` (item 224 — the location cue stays in item 220's own inline slot, unmoved, but reads along the diagonal spine's own rake instead of upright). Both variants carry a `LocationLabelStyle`: face (`Body` / `Mono` / `Chrome`), expression-relative scale, palette-role ink (`Flat` / `Gradient`), real shaper tracking in em, and locator grammar (`Label` / truthful indexed locator). Only placement and overflow remain expression mechanics — one `RotatedLabelPlacement`, decided in the dispatcher; text formatting, mask compose, cache, and fit solver are shared verbatim. Magpie authors its former chrome-face / 0.92 / muted→base-content / untracked label values as data, so its pixels stay unchanged. Its angle is DERIVED, never pinned: `render/chrome/diagonal.rs::location_axis_deg` reads the diagonal composition's own `ROW_STEP`/row-height ratio — and specifically the row's own MEASURED step (`DiagonalClusterRail::spine_step`, which a too-narrow card's `TRAVEL_MAX_BAND_FRACTION` yield can shrink below the authored constant), so the cue and the spine beside it cannot drift apart even under that yield. At Magpie's own near-vertical, non-quadrant angle the round trip measures contrast 0.65 at 1× and 0.83 at 2×, with worst-case departure from an ideal bilinear rigid rotation of 0.0046 of full coverage — the softening a slant costs, not ink the pipeline loses; supersampling was deliberately declined (it would buy at 1× what 2× already gives, at the cost of letterforms no longer matching their upright siblings). Laws: `render/tests/raked_location.rs`.

- **`RotatedRail` is composed against the ROOM, not the card** (`render/chrome/rotated_location.rs`'s `rotated_rail_placement`). The cue takes the wordmark placard's own outer MARGIN and sits just ABOVE it, while its authored scale remains relative to the placard's own type size. Cassowary authors `Mono`, `0.28`, `Flat(Muted)`, `0.06em`, and `IndexOnly { digits: 2 }`, producing a locator such as `03`: the number comes from the active mark's real one-based position in `overlay_lens`; an absent position parks rather than fabricating an index. The docked active tab owns the category label, so the rail does not repeat it. `COMMANDS` remains the composition's lone poster headline. The placard family is deliberately PHYSICAL (window-short-side scaled, zoom-independent), so the cue inherits that and does not change size with zoom or DPI.

  **The size IS the composition, so the cue draws at its authored scale or is absent** — never shrunk (`Overflow::Park`; `Raked` keeps `Overflow::Shrink`, whose budget is a neighbouring row's breathing room). It parks when there is no wordmark on the frame (a card in its fill regime, a title-less kind), when the wordmark hugs the room's ceiling rather than its floor (no shipping world does; a law fails by name if one asks), or when the margin cannot hold the run.

  **The responsive bound is measured from real rects, and the card's DRAWN span is wider than its box.** Along the reading axis the run may rise from the placard to the room's own top margin (that margin read off the placard's own inset, never re-stated); across it, it may not reach the card — and the bound there is `overlay_card_drawn_span`, not `card_x`, because under `Bars` the SELECTED row's plate grows OUTWARD past the card box (`grow_span`, mirrored on a right-anchored card) and its scrim pads that again. Laws: `render/tests/rotated_rail.rs` — authored hierarchy from both sides at both DPI tiers, the exact theme-data treatment, truthful indexing over every faceted lens, presence floors by ink EXTENT and by ink STRENGTH (a non-overlap law gets happier as its subject fades), non-overlap and no-clipping over every lens label in the roster, and a zoom sweep crossing into the park arm.

## Shared optical placement

`rotated_label::ink::InkBoundsCache` measures nonzero coverage by physical glyph
identity. `LabelMask::tight_ink` describes that visible drawing; `ink` and `size`
continue to describe the padded texture and its UVs. The shared geometry centers
visible ink inside a caller-owned slot. Exact quadrant rotations snap the pen to
the pixel grid; intermediate fold angles retain their continuous placement.

Chrome checkbox and disclosure controls use `control_marks` slots beside captions,
centered on the captions' visible ink. The UI tier reserves a 14-logical-pixel mark
slot and a 6-pixel caption gap. Checkboxes share the approved Awl Marks task pair;
disclosures use one Iosevka triangle turned right/down. Document list markers and
fold marks use the same cached ink measurement and centering while retaining their
own prefix geometry, type scales, theme drawings, hit targets and motion.
