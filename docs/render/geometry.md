## Adaptive-column placement (`render/geometry.rs`)

- On a small screen the symmetric centered column cramped the margin outline while the right margin sat empty. `TextPipeline::column_left` is the one owner of an adaptive policy (no config knob): shifts right under pressure to grant the outline a rail (taking the empty right-margin space), back to symmetric when there's room. Every downstream reader (caret/selection/washes, hit-test, drag handle, gutter) composes it for free.
- Pure policy `adaptive_column_left`, one formula `desired_left.min(max_left).max(symmetric_left)`, three regimes (wide = byte-identical passthrough to the old column; narrow = shift, width never touched; narrowest = re-centers → outline auto-hides at its existing floor). Hide threshold + shift threshold read the same `left` (can't drift). Transition is instant (glide banked). **Live-only:** the feel of a real small-screen resize.
- **THE RAIL RESERVATION KEYS ON THE WORKING SET, NOT THE CURRENT BUFFER (`TextPipeline::outline_wants_rail`).** The column belongs to the ROOM, not the document: if ANY open buffer would draw outline rows, the reservation holds across every buffer switch, and a buffer with no headings of its own simply draws nothing in the reserved margin. Keyed on the CURRENT buffer instead, a headed file and a heading-free one sat in different regimes and merely switching between them slid the whole page — column, gutter, margins — sideways by the rail's whole appetite (80px at the default measure on a 1200px window). Column geometry now moves only at moments the user caused: open, close, resize, outline/page toggle, measure change. The no-payoff guard is untouched (a room with no claimant reserves nothing) and the entry-ramp LERP still owns the resize axis. Animating the remaining legitimate transitions (the deliberately-banked glide) is a separate taste call, not done.
  - **One rule, three askers.** `outline::document_wants_rail(is_markdown, has_heading)` is the per-DOCUMENT half — deliberately free of every room-level fact. The draw gate (`chrome::outline::outline_layout`) and the reservation's active half (`TextPipeline::active_wants_rail`) ask it of the buffer on screen; `buffers::BufferRegistry::park` asks it of every OTHER open buffer. `outline_wants_rail` is the ONE place the room-level gates and the two halves combine. Census law: `render::tests::outline::the_rail_appetite_question_has_exactly_the_named_deciders` (per-file equality over the whole production tree, so a fourth site fails by name).
  - **No per-frame rescans.** `park` stamps the appetite once, over the buffer it is parking; `backgrounded_wants_rail` folds a handful of bools (the registry is capped). The stamp needs no version key: a parked buffer is immutable (`get` hands out `&Entry`, `take` REMOVES the slot), so the one transition that can change its text also ends the slot. Open/close/activate all move the aggregate through those doors; the ACTIVE buffer's half is re-derived from the shaped headings every sync, so typing a heading in or editing it out claims and releases the rail with no switch and nothing to invalidate.
  - **Both capture drivers answer it** through `CaptureSubject::set_wants_outline_rail` off the same shared `BufferRegistry`, so a `--keys` replay and a `--screenshot-app` run place the column identically; `CaptureOpts::fold_gutter` mirrors it onto the `ViewState`. The reservation also joins `blur_signature` — it can flip with no reshape behind it (a headed buffer closed from the working set), the same staleness class `page_on`/`measure` are hashed for.
- **THE CONTENT CLIP — `TextPipeline::content_clip` / `clip_rects_to_band` (`render/rects.rs`).** The ONE resolved region every document-content, selection-adjacent quad must paint inside: horizontally the writing column ALWAYS (`column_left()`..`+column_width()`), vertically the whole canvas on an ordinary frame narrowed to whatever region the document layer is actually drawing in (`doc_clip_band`). A drag whose hit-test clamps to the page's own left edge, or a relocated comparison scrolled so a selected/composing/caret row leaves the region, both resolve through this one rect — bounding PAINT only, never the SELECTABLE range. `range_rects` (feeding both `selection_rects` and `search_match_rects`), `preedit_rects`, and the caret's own park gate in `prepare_caret_layer` all route through it; on an ordinary frame this is a no-op (`doc_clip_band` is `None`, and selection/caret x is already inside the column by construction). The vertical bound used to come from the diff-as-preview panel's card rect — the single case where a document ever drew somewhere other than the whole canvas. A real relocated viewport (`comparison_viewport`, below) now supplies `doc_clip_band` as its vertical extent; the emitters, the one owner and the by-name sweep are untouched, and the X arm is genuinely exercised because the region moves horizontally too. Laws: `render/tests/selection_clip_law.rs` (a no-wildcard, by-name check that each emitter's own body calls the shared owner, plus the runtime proof the relocated case actually clips — its straddling canvas is now DERIVED from row geometry, not from the clip's own output, so a reverted clip fails on an escaping rect instead of on a fixture that stopped qualifying).
- **Decorative-overhang emitters get their OWN Y-only clip, `clip_decorative_rects_to_band`, never `clip_rects_to_band`.** A follow-up audit found `fence_panel_rects` and `code_pill_rects` — pre-existing DECORATIVE quads whose whole point is to overhang the bare glyph column (`FENCE_PANEL_INSET_X`/`CODE_PILL_INSET_X`, "so the panel reads as a distinct surface rather than being clipped exactly to the glyph edges") — routed through the strict writing-column X-clip meant for selection-adjacent quads. Invisible with page mode ON (the page pad dwarfs both insets); UNIVERSAL with page mode off (`text_pad()` is hard-zeroed then, so `text_left() == column_left()` and there is no margin to absorb the inset): every fenced block's panel clipped flush on both edges every frame, and an inline-code pill lost its left cap whenever the span touched column 0 or the wrap edge. Two distinctly-named owners apply: `clip_rects_to_band` (X+Y, strict writing-column bound) stays SELECTION-ADJACENT-only (`range_rects`, `preedit_rects`; the caret gate reads `content_clip` directly); `clip_decorative_rects_to_band` (Y-only) is the DECORATIVE owner — `fence_panel_rects`, `code_pill_rects`, and the column-bound `wash_rects` comment/string/highlight buckets (structurally safe either way, kept on the decorative owner for one consistent story). Laws: `render/tests/washes.rs`'s `fence_panel_overhang_survives_content_clip_with_page_mode_off` / `code_pill_left_inset_survives_content_clip_at_line_start_with_page_mode_off` (each with a page-off zero-slack precondition, page mode off); the four `render/tests/selection_clip_law.rs` tests still hold unmodified — the selection/search/preedit/caret X-clip is untouched by this fix.


## Toast placement (`render/plan/floating.rs`)

- `Theme::toast_anchor` chooses `TopLeft`, `TopRight`, or `BottomCenter`. No
  renderer names a world. `plan_toast` resolves that data into one text rect and
  one plate rect, applies logical safe/collision insets, and tries the small
  shared roster when the persistent outline, an active picker, or occupied
  workspace chrome takes the authored slot.
- A narrow canvas tries `BottomCenter` first once the two lateral slots no
  longer remain distinct around the measured plate. Elision still uses the
  notice buffer's real shaped width before placement. A HELD `Sticky` notice
  and a self-clearing `Toast` share the one authored `toast_anchor` and the
  same `plan_toast` collision/narrow-fallback planner — placement is a
  per-world axis, not a per-lifetime one; the two kinds differ only in plate
  ink (`notice_plate_inks`), never in where they sit.
- Laws cross 20 worlds × 3 anchors × document/picker/workspace ×
  narrow/ordinary/wide × 1×/2× (1080 pure geometry cells, kind-agnostic —
  `plan_toast` itself never reads a `NoticeKind`), then run the 720 production
  assigned-anchor cells (both notice kinds × outline/overlay/workspace × the
  same geometry sweep) against real outline/overlay/workspace plans. Five
  pixel-differential gallery cells prove the plated line is present, including
  Wagtail.


## The positional count cue (item 508, `render/chrome/overlay_clamp.rs`, `render/chrome/mod.rs`)

- **The problem it answers:** a windowed picker gave no indication of how much sat off-screen ("there is no scroll bar so like how do i even know where my files are"). DECIDED: no literal scrollbar — a faint text cue at the window's edges ("↑ 3 more" / "↓ 41 more"). This cue belongs to the flat and grouped candidate views (Go-to, the command palette, the theme picker, the destination navigators); the compact working set instead scrolls directly under the pointer. **The summoned workspace family (Settings, History, Conflict, Credits) is deliberately excluded** — see the dedicated bullet below.
- **One arithmetic owner, `render/chrome/mod.rs::window_edge_counts(top, visible, n_items)`** — reads `scroll_window`'s own `(top, visible)` pair and answers which edges the window clips, `(None, None)` when it doesn't. `visible` here is an ITEM count, never a display-line count: a sectioned card (the theme picker) windows DISPLAY LINES (section headers interleaved with item rows), but the cue counts hidden ITEMS — every geometry owner reads this at `item_top`/`item_visible` straight off `scroll_window`, BEFORE `window_plan` turns them into a display-line count that would double-bill every header as a hidden item.
- **THE RESERVATION IS SYMMETRIC AND SCROLL-INVARIANT — never "however many edges clip right now".** `scroll_window`'s own `visible` already ignores the scroll position (a pure function of the corpus size and the window's byte budget), so "is this corpus/canvas/query combination windowed at all" is itself scroll-invariant, decided once. Whether the CURRENTLY VISIBLE slice clips above, below, both or neither is not: scrolling from the top of a long list (clips only below) to its middle (clips both) changes which edges are non-empty every frame. An earlier cut of this mechanism charged exactly as many extra display lines as the edges clipping AT THE CURRENT scroll position — so the reserved overhead, and with it `first_top`/`card_h`/every real row's own Y, changed shape as the user scrolled through nothing but the same already-open card, caught by `render/tests/palette_scroll_anchor.rs`'s pre-existing "scrolling moves only the list, never the surface" law on an unrelated composition. The fix: `render/chrome/overlay_clamp.rs::resolve_window_and_cue` reserves BOTH edges unconditionally once the corpus is windowed at all — worst case, but the only value that cannot change with scroll — and reads the actual per-edge CONTENT (text or blank) off that fixed window afterward. `OverlayGeom` therefore carries the two facts separately: `cue_reserved: bool` (the scroll-invariant LAYOUT fact — is a line held open at each edge) and `cue_above`/`cue_below: Option<usize>` (the scroll-varying CONTENT — does this edge have something to say right now). `render/tests/edge_count_cue_law.rs::cue_card_geometry_is_scroll_invariant_while_windowed` is the direct regression law.
- **The cue may never be the thing that empties the window.** Reserving the two rows can push a family at its own `min_items: 0` starvation floor (the grouped card's own arm, `docs/render.md`'s height-clamp section above) to answer with zero items — so a corpus that comfortably showed a row without the cue could lose its LAST row to the cue's own reserved lines. `resolve_window_and_cue` detects this (the reserved pass's `visible` dropping to zero while the uncued pass had at least one) and falls back to the cue-free window; each geometry owner's own starvation-degrade arm (the existing "drop the hint gap rather than overrun the canvas" arm, extended) additionally drops the reservation when growing the card by the cue's rows alone would overrun the canvas.
- **Drawing rides existing chrome where one already stands, adds a genuinely new line only where none does.** The flat family's query-bearing card already carries a "beat" spacer line between the header and the first candidate (`push_beat_spacer`, the query-composition round); `plan.beat_line()` is derived as `first_top() - last.bottom()`, so once `plan_overlay_rows` shifts `first_top` for the cue's reservation, that derivation auto-inflates to include the cue's room — the above-edge cue's TEXT rides that same (now taller) line rather than opening a second one, and `TextPipeline::shaped_first_row_line` counts it only when NO such beat stands alone (a contextual card, or the grouped family, whose beat instead folds into the strip's own header box and so never "stands alone" at all). The grouped family (`theme_picker.rs::shape_theme_spans`) and a contextual card both push the cue as its own explicit line. Either way the line is present (blank or carrying text) whenever `cue_reserved`, so the reservation and the shaped line count agree on every family.
- **The summoned workspace family draws no cue, on purpose.** A `RailOverRows` workspace's (Settings) navigation RAIL — the primary column's category labels — shares its row origin (`first_top`, via the ONE shared `plan_overlay_rows`) with the CONTENT pane's candidate rows. The flat/grouped families' fix shifts that shared origin to reserve the cue's own room; wired onto the workspace family unmodified, it moved the rail's row 0 by a full row pitch every time the CONTENT pane's item count happened to clip — even though the rail itself never changed, and regardless of which category was selected. Caught immediately by the PRE-EXISTING `render/tests/rail_ink_law.rs::the_active_rail_entrys_label_reads_on_its_own_band_on_every_world` (photographs the same rail rect twice and requires the two shots to be identical). Decoupling the rail's own row origin from the content pane's cue reservation is real, separable future work; today `workspace.rs::workspace_geometry` never calls `resolve_window_and_cue` at all — `cue_above`/`cue_below`/`cue_reserved` are the base `None`/`None`/`false` unconditionally, and the family's item window is exactly its pre-508 `overlay_workspace_window` call, byte-identical.
- **Sidecar:** `overlay.window.cue_above` / `cue_below` (schema `/210`) — `null` on an edge with nothing hidden, else the item count, read off `TextPipeline::overlay_edge_cue_report`. See CAPTURE.md's own schema-history entry for the reader-facing arithmetic note.
- **Law:** `render/tests/edge_count_cue_law.rs` sweeps `OverlayKind::ALL` (no wildcard) at a tall-fits geometry (neither cue fires, and no line in the shaped buffer carries either cue's text — a presence-floor companion, not just an absence of the `Option`) and a corpus-forced-clip geometry (the per-kind cap binds, with the workspace family asserted to clip WITHOUT a cue rather than exempted from the sweep); two acceptance captures pin the command palette's default window (the per-kind cap axis) and the theme picker's own short window (the CANVAS axis — the geometry axis the item calls out explicitly, since either alone is the shape of law that would go green while blind to the other); and a scroll-invariance law over the flat and grouped families. The working-set expanded panel (`workingset/panel.rs`) has its OWN, separate windowing mechanism (`scroll`/`EXPANDED_VIEWPORT`, not `scroll_window`) — outside this law's roster sweep, which is `OverlayKind`-scoped.


## Picker rows (`render/rowlayout`)

Picker rows go through `render/rowlayout` — never place row text directly. A primary cell (never dropped, elided last-resort) + optional secondary right column (first to yield). `rowlayout::plan` → `fits` → `fit_primary` (the only elision door). The law test enumerates `OverlayKind` with a no-wildcard match. The bottom-left page-mode gutter rides the same owner (`gutter_plan`) — stacked, so neither line yields from width pressure (the filename never wraps; the fix for "DESIGN.md → DESIG/N.md and the project vanishes").


## The margin working set's active-file plate (`render/chrome/gutter_stack.rs`)

- **THE ACTIVE FILE IS ALWAYS PLATED — the lone identity line included** (user decision; it supersedes an earlier deliberate calm-when-alone contract that three rounds had kept, and which a reader hit head-on: one open file read as "not selected"). One owner answers "which line is filled", `gutter_stack::plate_rects`, over a no-wildcard match on `GutterLine`: `Name` (the lone file, active by construction — no working-set row exists to consult) and an active `File` row both plate; `Project` and `Changed` never do, and neither does a Group heading even when it is the current project (one plate per frame, or two fills answer two different questions and read as two selections). A new line kind fails to compile there rather than inheriting an answer.
- **The ink is a second shared owner, `gutter_stack::active_row_ink`** — `muted` routed through `theme::selected_row_secondary_ink` against the fill it sits on. That routing is the identity function in a minority of the roster, so the quiet ladder's own value survives there; across the rest `muted` falls under the contrast floor against `surface_selected` (down to 1.00 on Wagtail, where both are the same pure white, and 1.05 on Mulga) and a page pole is substituted. A plated line drawn in literal `muted` is therefore invisible across most of the roster — the reason the ink cannot simply be carried over from the unplated design.
- **Laws.** `gutter_stack::tests` for the geometry (the identity line's plate must equal the rect the same name gets as a stack row — a lookalike would drift on the first padding change) and the ink owner; `render/tests/gutter_stack_pixels.rs`'s `the_active_file_is_plated_alone_and_among_several_on_every_world` for the pixels, sweeping both cells the user's report names (one file open, and the newest of three) across the whole roster, with three independent floors: PRESENCE (the plate's interior is mostly one flat fill, and that fill sits a real distance from the identical rect one row up — a plate washed to `0x04` alpha measures 3.0 against a floor of 24), LEGIBILITY (the furthest interior pixel from the fill is the label's ink), FORWARDNESS (a ratio, active row against the block's quietest shipped line).
