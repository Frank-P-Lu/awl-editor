# Chrome composition

Read before changing these chrome mechanisms; use the render guide to find related owners.

[Guide](../render.md) · Paths in code examples are relative to the repo root.

- **`ListStyle::Ruled(RuleSelection)` — the QUIET fourth composition; Kite and Paperbark are its carriers (Paperbark first, Kite enrolled by user decision 2026-08-22).** It exists because `Pane` carried fifteen of twenty worlds while `Bars` and `Diagonal` between them covered only loud, so the missing grammar was a QUIET one. Its organising idea is ABSENCE: leading and hairline rules do the arranging and nothing is drawn as an object. Structurally that is `ListBacking::BarePlates` with `draws_row_plates()` FALSE — `Diagonal`'s own configuration, so no new seam was needed — plus one thing `Diagonal` did not need: `overlay_prepare_bar_scrims` asks `draws_row_plates()` before padding anything out, because a hairline grown by `BAR_SCRIM_PAD` on every side IS the plate this style refuses (`Diagonal` reached that code with an empty rect list and was scrimless by accident of that emptiness). The rules ride the EXISTING `overlay_bars`/`overlay_rows` quad pipelines — hairlines on the first, the selection mark on the second — so there is no new pass, no new uniform and no new shader. Its lengths are `RULE_HAIRLINE`/`RULE_SELECTED_WEIGHT`/`RULE_ROW_AIR`/`RULES_TEXT_HPAD`/`RULE_MARK_LEN`/`RULE_MARK_GAP` (`render/chrome/overlay_rules.rs`), all `Logical`; the air rides the ONE row-pitch owner `overlay_row_gap` exactly as a `Bars` plate gap does, and the gutter rides `overlay_text_hpad`. **`RuleSelection::Weight` is the shipped treatment** (chosen by the user against both rendered side by side): the two rules bounding the selected row thicken and run out past the text measure to the full band, and the row's interior stays plain ground. `RuleSelection::Gutter` leaves the row and its rules untouched and hangs a short heavy segment in the gutter beside it — and that segment SHORTENS into whatever gutter the band has rather than hanging off the card, because the contextual spell popup insets its text by its own `SPELL_PAD` rather than by the list style's. ⚠️ Neither may fill the row — a filled band is `Pane`'s answer and borrowing it would make this a restyle of that. Both are forceable for capture: `AWL_OVERLAY_LIST_FORCE=ruled[:weight|gutter]`.

- **`rules_ink` IS THE ONE OWNER of which rules a ruled list draws, and the WORKSPACE RAIL is one of them.** A rail is a list of labels arranged by the boundaries between them, which is exactly what this style says a list is; `prepare_rail_mark` therefore branches on `list_style` with no wildcard, and `Ruled` hands the rail to the same function the rows beside it come out of (hairlines on `overlay_facet_ghost`, the selection on `overlay_lens_underline`, the two pipelines the chip skins otherwise use). Its spans mirror the pane's: a hairline runs the LABEL measure — the rail column less the `2 * hpad` its own measurement reserves — and the selection's heavy rule runs the full column, which is the very rect the filled band occupied. **Every other style's rail keeps that band**, and the two regions still differ only in presence (`UNFOCUSED_MARK_ALPHA`), never in decoration. Before this the rail took the world's selected-row band unconditionally, so the carrier world shipped with its content pane ruled and its rail plated — a half-applied world.

- **The LENS STRIP's answer is a rule, not a pill.** A tab pill is a plate, so `overlay_strip_tab_plates` stays empty on `Ruled` (as it is on `Pane`) — and the strip is not thereby bare: `FacetStyle::Text` marks the active lens with a hairline under its label, which is the style's own vocabulary. ⚠️ The THEME picker is not where that question lives: its runtime lens strip was RETIRED (`facets.rs`, user decision 2026-07-15), so it carries no strip on any world. The strip surfaces are the file pickers, the Command palette, the History timeline and the Settings menu. **ITEM 289 (fixed):** `FacetStyle::Text`'s underline height and `ChipVariant::Underline`'s (Magpie's chip skin, not Wagtail — Wagtail is a `Text` world like the other thirteen) were raw device-pixel literals in `theme_picker.rs`, so both stayed put while every other term of the same rect doubled on a 2x panel (probed: `[430.0, 153.5, 23.9, 1.5]` at DPI 1, `[860.0, 306.6, 47.8, 1.5]` at DPI 2). Both are now `Logical` constants (`TEXT_MARK_THICKNESS`, `UNDERLINE_CHIP_THICKNESS`) resolved through `Metrics::px` like every other chrome length in the file — dpi-1 output is unchanged (still `1.5`/`3.5`), dpi-2 now doubles (`3.0`/`7.0`). Swept 1x/2x across the live roster (enrollment read from `render_caps.facet_style`, not a named list) in `render/tests/facet_mark_dpi.rs`.

- **`PaneSplit` (Split default | Unified) — the SPLIT-PANE composition (item 50).** A Pane world's takeover card draws as TWO surfaces: the title/query INPUT on the upper surface, a visible strip of the world's own background between, then ONE lower surface owning facets/section-headers + candidate rows + footer. DATA-gated through the ONE owner `render::effective_pane_split` (probe `AWL_PANE_SPLIT_FORCE=unified|split`); the fill rides `overlay_pane_fills` → `overlay_split_bounds` (the gap is carved from the query beat's own negative space, so NO text moves and no glyph falls in the gap — a pure fill change). Cassowary is the ONE `Unified` exception. Inert on a Bars world (bare plates) and the spell popup. Two bordered surfaces on a 1-bit world (Wagtail) read by their rims. Laws: `render/tests/split_pane.rs`.

- **Static summoned material is data, not animation.** `SummonedMaterial::Flat` is inert; `Scanlines { pitch_px, line_px, strength }` uploads bounded pane and placard rects through the shared selection shader. A `DockedTab` active facet continues that same transparent material through its true tab bounds, so the tab and pane share one absolute-y raster phase as well as one open outline; inactive docked labels use `faint`, leaving the `base_content` active label to carry the strip. The dials resolve through display density, carry no clock or randomness, and therefore schedule no redraws. Cassowary authors `4px / 1px / 0.12`; all other worlds remain Flat. `PlacardPlacement::Bleed { x_em, y_em }` is likewise a reusable post-anchor transform in the wordmark's own em; Cassowary uses it to crop the bottom of `COMMANDS` without changing the placard's reported geometry or the rotated rail's placement.

- **Held back, not dead:** Chips is rebuilt-for-real but ships inert pending the user's variant pick; poster facets stay Text. Probe forces for galleries: `AWL_FACET_STYLE_FORCE`, `AWL_OVERLAY_ANCHOR_FORCE` (env, CLI-invisible).

- **THE INTERIOR-RAIL PLACEMENT LAW (item 67) — one global resolver, `render::chrome::overlay::overlay_rail_inset(ww)`.** Supersedes the old flush ~28px `TopLeft`/`TopRight` edge-hug: the two side rails are now SYMMETRIC INTERIOR rails whose card CENTERS sit near the viewport's one-third / two-thirds marks when the card holds a comfortable width — generous outer breathing room, still unmistakably left/right. `TopCenter` is untouched (`free * 0.5`, exactly the viewport midpoint, always). The three theme-owned anchor CHOICES and every world's own assignment are unchanged; only the shared placement arithmetic moved. The inset is a PURE function of the window width alone — never the anchor, never the caller's actual `desired_w` — so `overlay_card_box_policy`'s `TopLeft` and `TopRight` arms read the exact same number and the left/right MIRROR law holds by construction, independent of content width (item 51's right-anchor content-hug still shares ONE right edge across a plain and a secondary-bearing card). The SAME `full -> anchored_max -> floor -> free` clamp chain the old fixed constant fed keeps the narrow-window collapse toward `CARD_EDGE_INSET_FLOOR` (then the re-centering fill regime) perfectly CONTINUOUS — no breakpoint jump, no per-anchor branch, no fourth user-facing anchor. `CardAnchor::Inset { x_frac }` keeps its exact `0.0 == TopLeft`, `0.5 == TopCenter`, `1.0 == TopRight` contract untouched (the same clamp expressions, just reading the new inset).

- **The card ANCHOR is frozen at summon but RE-STAMPED on a deliberate theme crossing (item 45 → item 52).** The render path reads the anchor ONLY through the frozen `resolve_overlay_anchor(self.overlay_align)` — never the live world (`render::tests::overlay_align_law`'s alignment-is-data grep-law bans `effective_card_anchor(`/`render_caps.card_anchor` anywhere under `src/render/`). Item 52 makes the theme picker CROSS its anchor like every other theme-owned property: `OverlayState::reanchor` (called by `actions::preview_move` on a keyboard nav / wheel move, and the wheel path in `app/input/mouse/overlay.rs`) re-stamps `align` to the highlighted world's anchor AFTER the preview makes it active, so the card SNAPS into the destination rail. Passive `overlay_hover` keeps the BARE `preview_overlay` (no reanchor — no spatial chase). The re-stamp lives in the overlay/actions layer, NOT a render consumer, so the grep-law still holds. Laws: `render::tests::reanchor_crossing_law`.

- **Right-anchored cards shrink to content (item 51):** a `TopRight` (`mirrors_growth`) takeover card no longer seats a wide `CARD_MAX_W` card against the right edge (the 300–400px dead middle between the left-aligned labels and the remote edge). `overlay_desired_w` (the ONE desired-width owner both geometry paths read) returns the MEASURED content width — `measure_overlay_content_w`, cached in `overlay_content_w` at sync via the SAME real shaper the draw runs (widest visible primary + optional secondary column + query/strip/footer + `2·hpad`), clamped to `[CARD_CONTENT_MIN_W, wide cap]` — so the whole group hugs the right window edge as one block, text still LEFT-aligned inside, the secondary right-aligned to the card's own text edge (a content-bounded scanning column, never the remote edge). Left/center keep `overlay_content_w == 0.0` → the fixed wide cap, byte-identical. Laws: `render::tests::overlay_right_hug_law`.

- **The flat card's width cap is `CARD_MAX_W = LogicalGrowOnly(545.0)`, raised from 520 to clear the Keybindings hint.** On Potoroo and Firetail — the two worlds whose CHROME face is `"Monaspace Xenon"`, and not monospace generally: nine other monospace-chrome worlds never clipped — that hint's shaped band exceeded the card's own text column, reading `esc clos` / `esc clo` instead of `esc close`. The budget side has no font term at all (a bare constant clamped to the window) while the content side is advances, so the fit is a ratio between two quantities that were never related; Firetail needed **1.0502** of its column and Potoroo 1.0121, and the two differ by 3.8 points of ratio because a `Bars` world's text hpad differs from a `Pane`'s. **545 clears both and 540 does not** — measured from both ends by the law below, which reports the whole sweep's overflow set as EMPTY at 545 and still names Firetail at 1.0080 at 540. Shortening the hint was never on the list: it is the discoverability affordance. ⚠️ **The cap is still `LogicalGrowOnly`, deliberately, and its floor is the DISPLAY.** Its `px` is `self.0 * scale.max(dpi)` — equivalently `dpi · zoom.max(1.0)` — so below ZOOM 1 it keeps its authored LOGICAL width while the text shrinks; at the authored 1.0 default and every zoom above it, the fit is a pure ratio. Which tier the cap is TUNED at is a separate question from whether the hint fits, and it is open. **The floor was a bare `1.0` until it was measured against the density.** `scale` is `zoom · dpi`, so a `1.0` floor bound for every zoom under 1 on a 1× panel and only under zoom 0.5 on a 2× one: at zoom 0.8 the same logical window drew a **545**-logical-px card for a 1× reader and a **436**-logical-px one for a 2× reader (`x` 27.5 → 82 logical on a `TopLeft` world; uniform −109 px on the 17 cap-bound worlds at every window size swept, and −8.8…−36.8 px on the content-hugging Mangrove, while Cassowary and Kite were already invariant because their content never reaches the cap). The elision budget — how much of a row's name is legible — was a property of the reader's panel. Every `dpi 1` capture and every `zoom ≥ 1` cell is byte-identical either way, which is why no law saw it: the one law grading this exact trade for the card ran at the capture door's default zoom of 1.0, the single value at which the two spellings agree. Law: `render/tests/foot_band_no_clip.rs` — 21 worlds × 18 carded kinds × 1×/2× × both menu-bar arms × zoom {0.8, 1.0} = 2880 graded cells, each kind through the geometry owner it really gets (the command palette FACETS, so its budget is the wider `CARD_MAX_W_FACETED` and never this one). **The final width is TASTE and owed to the user's eye: `gallery/item-338/`.**

- The theme picker retired its runtime lens strip (user decision 2026-07-15, recorded in src/facets.rs); the axes are a build-time ruler.

- **A SELECTED ROW'S FILL AND THE INK THAT READS ON IT ARE ONE DECISION, AND `chrome/overlay_visual_sel.rs` OWNS BOTH.** `overlay_selected_band_srgb` is the one resolution of `highlight_treatment(effective_overlay_selrow_band())`; `overlay_selected_primary_ink` (and its resolved form `overlay_selected_label_ink`, for a surface drawing one label on one band) is its twin. Four chrome sites each re-derived the FILL while only some asked this module for the INK, so a surface could take one without the other — and the summoned workspace's navigation rail did exactly that, laying the world's band under a category label shaped in unconditional `base_content`. On an inverse-video world the band IS `base_content`: measured on the real product, Wagtail's active Settings category was 2052 pixels of pure white with ZERO of them deviating — the label was absent, not faint. `render/tests/rail_ink_law.rs` holds both halves: a full-roster PIXEL law over the rail's own rect photographed twice (with its mark and without), pairing a label-vs-fill contrast floor with a label-PRESENCE floor and a band-PRESENCE floor so the ratio cannot be satisfied by fading the plate into the card; and a no-wildcard grep that no file under `render/chrome/` but the owner may name `effective_overlay_selrow_band` again. ⚠️ Two measurement traps that law records so they are not re-hit: a label graded as a SHARE of its own rect reads 5% wide and 0.7% staged-narrow with nothing wrong (the staged rail is the whole card wide), and a single modal fill for a rect assumes a flat ground the roster has not got (Paperbark's gradient, then Bilby's short rows, forced the background onto a per-column mode smoothed across a window).

- **THE THEME PICKER'S OWN CHROME IS PINNED FOR THE LIFE OF A SUMMON, WHILE EVERYTHING BEHIND IT PREVIEWS LIVE (user decision 2026-09-07, reader feedback: arrow-stepping the picker "made my boyfriend dizzy").** Every arrow used to re-run `effective_list_style`/`effective_facet_style`/`effective_pane_split`/`effective_card_anchor`/`effective_chrome_face`/the raw `render_caps.location_style` reads against `theme::active()`, and the theme picker's OWN preview step (`preview_overlay`, reached from every input kind through `preview_move`) is the one thing in the app that swaps `theme::active()` while an overlay stays open — so the card's anchor, list style, facet style, row pitch and visible-row window all re-composed into whatever world the reader had just arrowed onto, while the reader was trying to hold the selection with their eyes. The fix is one seam, not a per-composition special case: `render::picker_chrome_theme()` (a thread-local world-index pin, `render::pin_picker_chrome`/`unpin_picker_chrome`) is what those five `effective_*` resolvers (plus the new `effective_location_style`, which replaces every direct `render_caps.location_style` read in a render consumer) read INSTEAD OF `theme::active()` — so none of their ~40 existing call sites needs a kind-aware branch. `OverlayState::new_marked` pins on every `Theme` summon and unpins on every other kind's summon (exactly one overlay is ever open, and every kind passes through that one constructor, so this needs no dismiss/accept/revert hook). The pin is deliberately THREAD-LOCAL rather than a process-wide static like `theme::ACTIVE`: overlay construction is on the hot path of nearly the whole test suite, so a shared global here would put `crate::testlock::serial()` on every one of those tests; a thread-local behaves identically in the single-threaded production app and additionally isolates `cargo test`'s parallel worker threads from each other, which is what lets it skip the lock. `OverlayState::reanchor` (the DELIBERATE-crossing re-stamp an earlier round gave the anchor alone) is retired outright — the freeze now holds through a deliberate keyboard/wheel crossing exactly as it already held through a passive hover. Laws: `render/tests/theme_picker_chrome_pin_law.rs` (a full-roster arrow sweep holding card rect/list style/facet style/pane split/row pitch/window-capacity identical while the ground pixel outside the card changes, plus the mutation proof that dropping the pin lets the same crossing move the card), `render/tests/reanchor_crossing_law.rs` and `render/tests/overlay_hover_stability_law.rs` (the same claim against a real `OverlayState`, both crossing kinds).
