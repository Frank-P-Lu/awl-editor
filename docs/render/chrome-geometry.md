- **ITEM 314 — THE WRITING COLUMN'S LEFT EDGE IS A LOGICAL QUANTITY (307's promotion candidate, landed).** `TEXT_LEFT` was an untyped `f32` doing duty as a *layout* offset while every scaled term around it carried the display factor, so at a MATCHED LOGICAL window the column's left edge moved with the DPI. Reproduced two ways before touching anything: analytically `desired_left_logical = 228.96 + 16/dpi` (`18 chars × 14.4 × LABEL 0.8 = 207.36`, `gap 21.6`), and empirically at logical 1200×800 — plateaus **244.96 / 236.96 / 234.29** at dpi 1/2/3, a collapsed page pinned at **16 / 8 / 5.33** logical px, and the gutter's own boundary at **75/76** at 1x versus **76/77** at 2x and 3x. **The census found ONE role, not two:** the physical duty 307 attributed to the constant belongs to `adaptive_column_left`'s closing `.floor()` (the subpixel-shimmer snap), which floors whatever the policy returns — `TEXT_LEFT`'s integrality is not load-bearing for it, and scaling the pad leaves the snap exactly as it was. Every reader wants the same logical length, so the fix is enrolment rather than a split: `TEXT_LEFT`, `NONPAGE_INSET` and `PAGE_MIN_MARGIN_PX` are now `Logical`, and the newtype (item 242's family) makes the old bug unrepresentable — the compiler enumerated all 45 call sites. The pads resolve against **`self.dpi` alone, never `Metrics::scale`**, because the page column lives in `page_column_advance`'s zoom-stripped space and its margins are zoom-invariant by design. `adaptive_column_left`'s `left_pad` PARAMETER is gone, replaced by `dpi`: a caller can no longer hand the policy an unscaled pad. `TextPipeline::edge_pad()` is the one pipeline-side resolution, shared by the column, the outline's rail inset and the outline's click band, so the rail is clickable exactly where it draws. **Two things this measured that the fix does not change:** `page_min_margin`'s upper bound is provably INERT in `column_width_for` (0 of 3996 swept cells change if it is removed — whenever it binds the clamp, the measure already fits and the margin never shows), and `column_left_for`'s `.max(pad)` is a second, independently sufficient collapse guard, so each collapse site alone is compensated and a single-site mutation stays green. **Consequence a human should look at:** on a Retina display the page's collapsed side pad and the rail inset now sit at 16 LOGICAL px where they sat at 8 — the intended value, doubled in physical terms, and the same class as `readout::CANVAS_INSET`'s recorded promotion. `TEXT_TOP` (16.0) is the untouched vertical twin: still an untyped `f32` read unscaled by `doc_top`/`visible_lines_z`/`scroll.rs`, and the same defect on the other axis. **Laws:** `render/geometry/tests.rs`'s `adaptive_column_left_is_dpi_invariant_at_matched_logical_geometry` (pure; 4 tiers including fractional 1.5 × 9 logical widths from the true 464 minimum × measures 10–120 × page on/off × rail on/off, asserting the PRE-SNAP position exactly and the snapped one within one physical px, **with a companion PRESENCE floor** because invariance alone is satisfied by deleting the pad — `0 × dpi` is perfectly invariant) and `render/tests/column_left_dpi.rs` (four pipeline claims: column/text-origin invariance, drawn↔hit-test agreement, the rail's click band at one logical x asserted on BOTH sides, and the gutter boundary with a rail present — which 307's own law could not see because its fixture has no headings, so `outline_wants_rail()` is false and the policy is a passthrough). Non-vacuity: the full pre-fix state fails the gutter law with the reported numbers verbatim (`Some(75)` at dpi 1 versus `Some(76)` at dpi 2). The drawn↔hit law survives every pad mutation BY DESIGN — both sides compose one owner — and is red only against a parallel geometry; its doc says so.

- **ITEM 315 — `TEXT_TOP` IS `TEXT_LEFT`'S VERTICAL TWIN, AND THE DECLARATION LAW'S SCOPE IS WHY BOTH SURVIVED.** `TEXT_TOP` was the last untyped `f32` in item 242's chrome-pad family, read unscaled by `doc_top`, `max_scroll_rows`, `visible_lines_z`, `zoom_anchor_target_top` (`render/geometry.rs`) and `viewport_avail_px` / `char_screen_top_scroll` / `hit_test_scroll` (`render/scroll.rs`) — the SAME "unscaled pad mixed with scaled terms" shape 314 fixed one axis over, unconditional on the document's default path (`doc_top`'s `None` arm), so — unlike `TEXT_LEFT`'s rail branch — no heading-bearing fixture was needed to reach it. Fixed the same way: `TEXT_TOP` enrols in the `Logical` family, and the census that followed (45 readers for `TEXT_LEFT`; a comparable spread here across `render.rs`, `render/geometry.rs`, `render/scroll.rs`, `render/text.rs`, `render/chrome/{hud,outline,readout}.rs`, `app/lifecycle.rs`, `app/input/keys.rs`, `capture/sidecar.rs`) is genuinely LOGICAL everywhere — no second physical role. **The repeated idiom `TEXT_TOP + menubar_reserve()` had SIX independent spellings** (`doc_top`, the zoom anchor, both `scroll.rs` fns, the margin Outline, the capture sidecar) before this item — the reason the un-scaled constant survived unnoticed at half of them even after `menubar_reserve` existed. Consolidated into one owner, `TextPipeline::text_origin_top()` (`self.metrics.px(TEXT_TOP) + self.menubar_reserve()`), and every one of the six now calls it instead of re-deriving the sum; `zoom_anchor_target_top`'s `menubar: f32` parameter was renamed to `text_origin_top: f32` and its own `TEXT_TOP` reference deleted, so the pure fn cannot independently forget the scale multiply. `corner_origin`'s `CornerAnchor::TopCenter` arm — the one place a chrome-level fn (not a `TextPipeline` method, so no `Metrics` to multiply by) needed the composed value — now takes it pre-composed from its ONE caller (`prepare_notice`) rather than adding `TEXT_TOP` internally; every other anchor is unaffected.

  **ITEM 322 — `src/render.rs` IS NOW SWEPT, AND THE CENSUS FOUND 37 UNTYPED CONSTANTS WHERE ~30 WERE EXPECTED. Item 315 declined to guess and was right to: the file holds FOUR families, and one of them turned out to be a live defect at four times the count of the constants this item was written to classify.** Each family now has a mechanism rather than a judgement, which is the whole point — a constant mis-declared `Logical` gets multiplied by DPI forever with a law asserting it is right.
  - **Eleven base text/caret metrics are resolved by `Metrics::with_dpi` itself** (`FONT_SIZE`, `LINE_HEIGHT`, `CHAR_WIDTH`, `CARET_W/H/BLOCK_H`, the four `CARET_STREAK_*`, `CARET_TRAIL_TEXT_CENTER_DROP`): the owner multiplies each by `s = zoom * dpi` and stores the result on `Metrics`, which every consumer reads. Declaring one `Logical` and passing it through `Metrics::px` as well would apply DPI TWICE — invisible at the `--capture-dpi 1` every capture defaults to. **The law derives this set by parsing `with_dpi`'s own body** (`metrics_resolved_constants`), so the exclusion expires the day the owner stops multiplying one; mutating `font_size: FONT_SIZE * s` to `font_size: FONT_SIZE` makes `FONT_SIZE` an offender by name.
  - **`PAGE_TEXT_PAD_CHARS` is a `Chars`** — resolved against `metrics.char_width`, already scaled, so it must never also pass `Metrics::px`. The family existed; the constant had simply never joined it.
  - **`CARET_DESCENDER_PAD` and `CARET_INK_PAD` are `Logical`.** Their caret call sites recover the full factor from the metrics themselves — `px = m.caret_h / CARET_H` is exactly `Metrics::scale`, because `with_dpi` built `caret_h` as `CARET_H * s` — and now hand it to `Logical::px`. Same multiply, so every scale is byte-identical.
  - **A new `Millis` newtype carries the three animation durations** (`COPY_PULSE_MS`, `OVERLAY_ENTRANCE_MS`, `OVERLAY_BAND_SLIDE_MS`). A millisecond is not a length and the TYPE says so: `Millis` has no `Metrics::px`, so the compiler — not a table a reader maintains — is what keeps a duration out of a pixel multiply (`self.metrics.px(OVERLAY_ENTRANCE_MS)` fails with *expected `Logical`, found `Millis`*). Its one arithmetic is `progress_per(dt)`. **This is the by-KIND exclusion the ratio family still lacks**: `PAGE_MIN_MARGIN_FRAC`, `COPY_PULSE_LIFT_L`, `COPY_PULSE_LIFT_ALPHA` and `CARET_MORPH_SETTLE_SHOW` are recorded in the law's DIMENSIONLESS table with their reasons, which is a name list guarded only by an exhaustiveness assertion; `Ratio`/`Alpha` newtypes would put them on the same footing as `Millis`.
  ‼ **THE SIXTEEN WRITING-COLUMN DECORATIONS THAT HELD THEIR DEVICE SIZE ARE NOW `Logical`, AND THE LEDGER THAT HELD THEM IS GONE.** `metrics.zoom` is the clamped user zoom; `metrics.scale` is `zoom * dpi`. Sixteen constants multiplied by `.zoom` ALONE (and `OVERLAY_ENTRANCE_DROP_PX` by nothing at all), so a spell squiggle's amplitude and period, a table's cell pad, column gap and two rule thicknesses, an inline-code pill's overhang, a fence panel's inset, the preedit underline, the I-beam's width, the space-bar caret's width, the morph dilation and the overlay's entrance drop all held their DEVICE size as the display got denser — rendering at half their tuned size beside doubled text on every retina panel, the same defect chrome carried one neighbourhood over. **Measured, not inferred**, at identical logical geometry (1200x800): the inline-code pill was `92.70`px wide at dpi 1 and `179.39`px at dpi 2, where twice the first is `185.39` — short by exactly `2 x CODE_PILL_INSET_X`; the fence panel short by exactly `2 x FENCE_PANEL_INSET_X`. All sixteen are `Logical` and every read site passes `Metrics::px`, including the four in `chrome/preview.rs` / `chrome/popover.rs` a file partition once put out of reach. **1x is unchanged** — the factor only differs at `scale != 1` — and `DPI_BLIND_PENDING` is deleted along with the leftover-set cross-check that graded it: with nothing left over, a bare `f32` in the swept sources now fails the declaration law itself with nowhere to be parked. **The type is what closes it, not a table**: `Logical` carries no arithmetic, so `SPELL_AMP * m.zoom` is a compile error rather than a lint — the repair enumerated its own remaining sites as twelve build errors across five test files.
  **The outcome half of that repair is its own law**, because a declaration sweep grades the constant and only a geometry law grades the FACTOR its read site hands it: `render/tests/writing_column_decor_dpi.rs` measures the pill's inset (both axes), the fence panel's overhang (both edges) and the squiggle's amplitude/period/stroke at four densities including fractional 1.5, each pinned against a HARD-CODED authored value with the display factor divided back out — a pin that re-read the constant would pass under a `Logical(0.0)` mutation of it — and each with a presence floor, since invariance alone is satisfied by deleting the decoration. ⚠️ **Its fixture hazard is worth knowing:** raising the DPI of a pipeline that already holds a shaped document leaves `visual_rows`'s own `line_height` at the value it shaped at (32.0 at dpi 1, 1.5, 2 and 3 alike), so a band derived from the row height measures against a stale row while its x-extents track the new metrics. The law builds a FRESH pipeline per tier with the DPI set before the document — the capture path's own order — and a first draft that re-used one pipeline reported a pill band that SHRANK with density, which was the fixture and not the product.
  **The missing law this audit wrote:** `no_length_is_resolved_against_zoom_alone`. The newtype stops a length reaching a draw call UNMULTIPLIED; it does not stop a caller handing `Logical::px` the wrong factor, since `px` takes any `f32` (the pure policies are called with a bare `dpi`). So the factor is now a law — `Metrics::scale`, or the `dpi` a policy was handed, never `zoom` alone — swept over every product source, because the caret's own pads live outside the declaration files. 46 scale-taking resolutions graded, with a floor asserting the scan found them; `CARET_DESCENDER_PAD.px(m.zoom)` fails it by file and line.
